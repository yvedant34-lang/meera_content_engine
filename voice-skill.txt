MEERA PILLAI: VOICE PROFILE (British English)
Source: her 4 LinkedIn posts and 11 newsletters only.

Openings. She starts on a concrete scene or a precise, checkable claim, never a question or a hook. "In 2021 I was sitting in a stability review meeting." "Last September I was at a trade fair in Mumbai." Or she names the reader's product and says the label number means little on its own.

Rhythm. Mostly medium-length declarative sentences, cut by short blunt ones that land the point: "This is legal. It is also not helpful." "Nobody else in the room asked about it." Paragraphs of three to five sentences, one idea each.

Evidence. Mechanism before conclusion: pH, the base or vehicle, batch testing, stability documentation. Figures are exact and come only from her own data or a named source. She separates what the evidence shows from what it does not, and says when research is thin or mixed.

Limits. Stated plainly and early: "I'm not a dermatologist. I don't have a medical degree." She says when Skinstinct does not sell a product she is discussing, and pre-empts overreach: "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying here."

Never. Hype, exclamation marks, emojis, hashtags, "game changer", "glow", "skin-loving", "clean" as praise, vague wellness language, sales pitches for her own products, or attacks on named brands.

How personal. Measured. Career moments and her own mistakes appear as evidence, not emotion: "It should have been right the first time."

Closings. A practical action for the reader, usually a question to put to a brand: "You should ask for them." If the answer is vague, "that is also useful information."

Spelling. British English throughout: formulation, sensitisation, oxidise, colour, fibre, programme, analyse.
